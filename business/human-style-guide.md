# /human Style Guide for AI-Generated Social Content

Use these rules when generating social content with Gemini. The goal is to sound like a real developer, not a marketing bot.

## Core Rules

1. **Be specific, not vague**
   - Bad: "Built a cool app with modern tech"
   - Good: "Shipped a Stripe checkout flow with retry logic after 3 failed payments"

2. **Show, don't tell**
   - Bad: "I'm a passionate full-stack developer"
   - Good: "I just reduced API latency from 800ms to 120ms by switching to connection pooling"

3. **One idea per sentence**
   - Bad: Long paragraphs with multiple topics
   - Good: Short sentences. One thought. Then another.

4. **No jargon soup**
   - Bad: "Leveraged synergies across the full-stack paradigm"
   - Good: "Used Next.js API routes + Postgres to avoid a separate backend"

5. **Include a real detail**
   - Mention a specific error you hit
   - Mention a specific library or pattern
   - Mention a number: time saved, lines removed, percent faster

6. **End with a question or CTA**
   - "Has anyone else tried this approach?"
   - "Feedback welcome"
   - "Repo linked in comments"

7. **Hashtags: 2-3 max, relevant only**
   - Bad: #coding #developer #programmer #tech #software #webdev #javascript (7)
   - Good: #NextJS #PostgreSQL #FullStack (3)

8. **Emoji: 0-1 max, optional**
   - Bad: 🚀🔥💯✨🚀🔥
   - Good: One emoji if it fits, otherwise none

## Platform Rules

| Platform | Length | Tone | Format |
|----------|--------|------|--------|
| LinkedIn | <300 words | Casual professional | Short paragraphs, whitespace |
| Twitter/X | 5-7 tweets | Honest builder | Thread format, hook first |
| Instagram | <2200 chars | Excited but real | Line breaks, question at end |
| Blog | 500 words | Practical | Outline with decisions and gotchas |

## Anti-Spam Signals to Avoid

- "I'm excited to announce..."
- "I'm thrilled to share..."
- "Feel free to reach out..."
- "Don't forget to like and subscribe"
- Mass tagging
- Generic hashtags like #coding, #developer

## Human Touches to Include

- A mistake you made and fixed
- A "hot take" or opinion
- A question to the reader
- A specific tool/library you discovered
- A real metric or result
